How Much Does a Solar System Cost in Bulgaria in 2026, and Is It Worth Investing?

July 6, 2026 8 min read Sunlytics team

In 2026, the solar market in Bulgaria is more mature than ever. Equipment prices have dropped nearly 40% over the past five years, and Bulgaria's strong sunshine hours mean systems produce more per installed kilowatt than in most of the EU. Still, many people don't know where to start, or what the real price and payback actually look like once you factor in Bulgaria's electricity prices and its lack of residential net metering, as opposed to a marketing brochure figure.

This article is for anyone seriously considering a solar system who wants to understand the numbers before talking to installers.

Key factors that determine a solar system's price

Before getting into specific numbers, it's worth understanding what actually drives the price. In our experience, two properties with similar roofs can end up with very different prices for a few reasons:

  • System power (kWp): the larger the system, the lower the price per kilowatt.
  • Roof type: flat roofs are usually more expensive to install on than pitched roofs, due to the need for special mounting structures.
  • Roof orientation and shading: a south-facing roof with no shade is ideal. East- or west-facing roofs reduce efficiency by 15-25%.
  • Distance and infrastructure: the further the inverter is from the electrical panel, the more cabling and labor cost.
  • Inverter type: string inverters are cheaper, but modular options (microinverters or optimizers) work better on shaded roofs.

Methodology note: annual output assumes roughly 1,300 kWh per installed kWp per year, based on PVGIS data for the Sofia area. Savings are calculated using a blended electricity value that combines self-consumed power (around €0.13-0.15/kWh, near the bottom of the EU range) with exported surplus, which is bought back at a much lower regulated feed-in rate (~€0.04/kWh) since Bulgaria does not currently have residential net metering. Systems sized for higher self-consumption pay back faster; systems that mostly export surplus pay back more slowly.

Will a solar system pay off in Bulgaria?

Short answer: yes, though payback tends to run a bit longer than in EU countries with higher electricity prices or a working net-metering scheme. Bulgaria has some of the lowest household electricity prices in the EU and doesn't yet credit exported surplus at the retail rate — but it also gets significantly more sunshine than most of Europe, which offsets part of that gap. The economics work best when you size a system for your own consumption rather than maximum output.

Let's compare solar with other investments:

  • Bank deposit in 2026: 2-3% annual return
  • Rental apartment in Sofia: roughly 5-7% gross annual yield
  • Solar system: 8-14% annual return (depending on self-consumption and the property)

A solar system also increases property value. Studies across Europe show that homes with solar panels sell for 4-6% more on average.

Commercial properties: why it's an even better investment

In a residential home, most of the electricity generated by a solar system gets used in the evening, at night, or in winter, when the system isn't producing. Since Bulgaria doesn't yet have net metering, that surplus gets bought back at a low regulated feed-in rate instead of being credited at the retail price — which makes self-consumption timing matter even more here than in countries with net metering.

In a commercial building, it's the opposite: electricity gets used during the day, exactly when the system produces the most. That means almost all the generated energy is consumed on-site immediately at the full retail rate, and payback can be as short as 6-7 years.

Real-world example: a warehouse or production facility with a 50 kWp system, operating weekdays 8am-6pm, typically pays back in 6-7 years and generates roughly €120,000-€180,000 in net profit over 25 years, depending on consumption.

Common mistakes when choosing a solar system

  • Choosing based on price alone. The cheapest system often uses lower-quality panels that produce 15-20% less electricity after 10 years. Think in terms of a 25-year outlook.
  • Skipping a roof analysis before buying. Shade from a chimney or a neighboring house can cut system efficiency by 20-40%. It's important to know this before investing.
  • Forgetting about maintenance. Solar panels need minimal upkeep (cleaning once a year), but inverters can occasionally fail and need repair. Negotiate the warranty terms.
  • Not coordinating with your local DSO early. Grid connection can take 2-6 months. Start this process as early as possible.
