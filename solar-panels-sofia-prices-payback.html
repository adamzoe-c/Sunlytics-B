Solar Panels in Sofia: Prices, Payback and What You Need to Know in 2026

July 5, 2026 7 min read Sunlytics team

Solar energy in Bulgaria is no longer just a futuristic idea. In 2026, dozens of companies across the country offer photovoltaic systems, and interest among Sofia residents and business owners is growing fast, helped by Bulgaria's strong sunshine hours. But most people who start looking into solar panels run into the same problem: it's unclear how much it actually costs.

This article gives you a real, up-to-date price overview, without the marketing tricks.

What makes up the price of a solar system?

Many people assume the price of a solar system is just the cost of the panels. In reality, that's only part of the total. Here's what makes up the price of a typical 10 kWp system:

  • Solar panels make up about 35-40% of the total cost. A quality 500W monocrystalline panel costs around €90-120 in 2026.
  • The inverter is the device that converts DC power to AC. Price depends on power and manufacturer, averaging €800-3,000.
  • The mounting structure (roof fixing system) makes up about 10-15% of the price.
  • Installation labor makes up another 15-20%. An experienced team in Sofia charges around €150-350 per day, generally lower than in Western Europe.
  • Electrical work and grid connection through your local distribution operator (DSO) costs €400-1,200 depending on the site.
  • Permits and technical documentation cost an additional €250-500.

How long does payback take in Sofia?

This is the question everyone asks. The answer depends on three things: the system's price, the size of your electricity bill, and how much of your own production you use directly rather than export to the grid.

According to PVGIS data, the average annual solar irradiation in the Sofia region is about 1,650 kWh/m² at the optimal panel angle — notably higher than in most of northern Europe. This means:

  • A 10 kWp system in Sofia produces about 12,500 - 13,500 kWh per year.
  • Bulgaria has some of the lowest household electricity prices in the EU (around €0.13-0.15/kWh in 2026), and unlike some EU countries it does not yet have residential net metering — surplus exported to the grid is bought back at a much lower feed-in rate, around €0.04/kWh. For a well-sized system with strong self-consumption, that typically works out to savings of roughly €1,100 - €1,600 per year.
  • A system costing €11,000 - €14,500 would have a payback period of about 8-10 years.
  • After that, the system produces electricity almost for free for another 15-20 years.

Good to know: modern solar panels are guaranteed to retain at least 80% of their power output after 25-30 years. That means your investment will keep paying off for years beyond the payback period itself — and sizing your system for self-consumption, rather than maximum output, meaningfully shortens payback since Bulgaria doesn't credit surplus at the retail rate.

Is there government support in Bulgaria?

Not as much as in some other EU countries, and it's worth understanding the gaps before you budget:

  • No net metering (yet): Bulgaria does not currently have a residential net-metering scheme. A "virtual net metering" bill has been under discussion in the National Assembly, but as of 2026 it has not been enacted. Surplus electricity is bought back at a low regulated feed-in rate instead of being credited at the retail price.
  • Household subsidy program mostly closed: a scheme financing up to 70% of residential PV costs launched in 2023, but the Ministry of Energy confirmed in 2025 that it would not fund a second round, so it's largely unavailable to new applicants. Check with the Ministry of Energy or your municipality for the latest status.
  • No VAT relief: Bulgaria applies its standard 20% VAT rate to solar equipment and installation — there's no reduced rate for solar, unlike some neighboring EU countries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do solar panels work well in Bulgaria's climate?

Yes, and better than in most of Europe. Bulgaria gets significantly more annual sunshine than northern EU countries, with the south of the country among the sunniest regions in the bloc. Panels still generate electricity on cloudy days, and Sofia's irradiation levels are well above places like Vilnius or Berlin.

Do you need a permit to install solar panels?

For most residential self-consumption systems up to 10 kWp, a simplified connection procedure applies rather than a full building permit. Larger or commercial installations need approval from your local distribution operator (DSO) and may require additional permits. Confirm the exact requirements with your installer, since Bulgaria's rules for smaller systems have been evolving.

What's the difference between mono- and polycrystalline panels?

Monocrystalline panels are more efficient (21-22% vs 17-18%) and perform better in low light, though they cost a bit more. The price gap has narrowed significantly by 2026, so we recommend choosing monocrystalline.
